Plan Situations

Two Common Retirement Plan Conversations

Some owners are reviewing a plan that already exists. Others are considering whether a new plan belongs in the company’s benefit strategy. Either way, the conversation starts with the business — not a product.

You Already Offer a Plan. Now You Want a Clearer Picture of the Process Behind It.

Why This Matters
A retirement plan is not a one-time setup. Plan sponsors are generally expected to maintain a prudent, documented process over time. In an environment where ERISA litigation and regulatory scrutiny remain important considerations, it can be useful to understand how the plan is reviewed, who is involved, and what documentation exists.

A review does not assume something is wrong. It is a way to understand what is already in place, what is being handled, and which planning dimensions may deserve discussion.

  • ✓
    Understand the review history — We can help review the plan’s investment process, costs, provider roles, and documentation history in plain English.
  • ✓
    Discuss plan sponsor responsibilities — We explain where plan sponsor duties generally show up and what a documented review process may include.
  • ✓
    Consider a repeatable review rhythm — The goal is not a one-time report; it is to discuss a process the business can evaluate and maintain over time.
  • ✓
    Review authority with advisory support — Under a written 3(21) agreement, Brent can support the investment advisory process while the plan sponsor retains final decision-making authority.

Planning Situations

Three Situations Worth Discussing

These examples describe planning conversations and possible work products. They are not promises of a specific result or statements about your current plan.

Existing Plan · Periodic Review
A plan that has been running for years where the owner wants a clearer view of the process behind it.
The Planning Concept
A company retirement plan is maintained through process: periodic review, clear records, cost comparison, and a rationale for decisions. The technical terms are IPS review, fee benchmarking, fund lineup monitoring, and fiduciary documentation — but the business issue is simpler: the owner may want to understand what is being reviewed and why.
In this type of engagement, we can review the plan’s current support structure, available documentation, provider roles, plan costs, and investment review process. The review may confirm that the plan is already well supported, or it may identify planning dimensions worth discussing further.
Possible Work Product
A plain-English review summary and a proposed review calendar for the business owner to evaluate.
Illustrative example only. Individual plan situations vary. This is not legal or tax advice.
New Plan · First 401(k)
A company considering whether a retirement plan fits its workforce and owner planning goals.
The Planning Concept
A new retirement plan should be evaluated in the context of the business, employees, and owner. Design choices may affect workforce goals, employer contributions, payroll coordination, tax credit eligibility, and responsibilities after launch.
In this type of engagement, we can compare design considerations, coordinate with the appropriate providers, review the SECURE Act 2.0 tax credit structure with your tax professional, and discuss the ongoing plan sponsor process before the plan goes live.
Possible Work Product
A new-plan discussion framework with provider roles, design considerations, owner decisions, and a proposed implementation timeline.
Illustrative example only. Individual plan situations vary. This is not legal or tax advice.
Bundled or Legacy Plan · Clarification
A plan created through payroll, a provider, or a prior relationship where the owner wants clarity.
The Planning Concept
Some plans are created through bundled platforms or prior relationships. The question is not whether a provider exists; it is whether the owner understands who handles investments, administration, payroll coordination, employee support, and ongoing review.
In this type of situation, we map the current provider structure, review available plan documents, identify who is responsible for each function, and clarify where advisory support fits.
Possible Work Product
A responsibility map and documentation checklist designed to help the plan sponsor evaluate how the plan is supported.

Our 3(21) Approach

Advisory Support for
Plan Sponsor Decisions.

A 3(21) advisory relationship is built for business owners who want qualified support while retaining final authority over the plan. Under a written agreement, JCSB supports the investment advisory process as a co-fiduciary while the plan sponsor remains the final decision-maker.

In practice, that can include research, monitoring, provider conversations, cost comparisons, and documentation support. The plan sponsor reviews the recommendations and makes decisions with a clearer process in front of them.

The Bigger Picture

Your Company Plan Connects to Your Personal Wealth.

For many business owners, the retirement plan is both an employee benefit and a meaningful component of the owner's long-term wealth strategy. The decisions are not separate.

Owner Contribution Strategy

Contribution design, salary vs. distribution mix, and owner deferral amounts interact with personal tax planning. The plan sponsor's decisions affect the owner's personal financial position — not just the employee benefit.

Tax Planning Coordination

Plan design affects the owner's personal tax situation — including potential deductions, contribution timing, and the interaction between company cash flow and personal income planning.

Exit and Succession Context

For business owners planning an eventual sale or transition, the retirement plan structure may affect workforce planning, buyer discussions, and the owner's personal distribution strategy.

This is not tax or legal advice. The planning concepts above describe dimensions that may be worth coordinating with a financial advisor, CPA, and legal counsel. Consult qualified professionals for your specific situation.

Planning Dimensions We Help Organize

The Technical Work, Explained in Business Terms

Once the business owner understands why the plan matters, the ongoing work becomes easier to discuss: review the benefit, document the process, and coordinate the moving parts. These are planning dimensions that may be covered on a scheduled basis, depending on the engagement.

📄
Investment Policy Statement Review
Periodic review to evaluate whether the IPS still reflects the plan's structure, participant profile, and investment objectives. Documentation may include the rationale for any changes.
📊
Fee Benchmarking
Plan costs can be compared against relevant market data on a scheduled basis. The comparison and rationale may be documented for the plan's file.
🔍
Investment Lineup Review
Fund options may be reviewed against IPS criteria. Any recommendations to retain, replace, or add options should be supported by documented rationale.
👥
Participant Education Support
Coordination of participant-facing communication may help the plan sponsor explain plan mechanics more clearly.
🔗
Provider Coordination
Liaison with TPA, recordkeeper, and payroll on service questions, plan amendments, and administrative items, depending on the scope of the engagement.
🗂️
Fiduciary Meeting Documentation
Review sessions may be documented so the plan sponsor has a record of decisions discussed, rationale provided, and advisors consulted over time.

The scope and frequency of advisory services depend on the terms of the written advisory agreement. The above is illustrative of the types of activities that may be included.
