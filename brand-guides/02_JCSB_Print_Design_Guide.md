\# JCSB Wealth Management — Print Design Guide ### Medium-Specific Reference for All Print & Static Document Work *Last updated: March 2026 — Internal Use Only* --- ## How This Document Works This guide covers everything specific to print and static documents. It **extends** the Brand Foundation (`01_JCSB_Brand_Foundation`) — it does not redefine colors, typefaces, or brand philosophy. Load the Brand Foundation first, then this guide. **Use this guide for:** One-pagers, leave-behinds, pitch documents, white papers, comparison sheets, proposal documents, and any material rendered as a static file for print or PDF delivery. The core rule that separates print from web: **print is a static, physical medium.** It has no hover states, no light source, no interactivity. Every design decision that works on screen because of motion, depth, or user feedback must be reconsidered — often eliminated — for print. Many things explicitly avoided here are actively encouraged in the Web Design Guide. --- ## 1. Design Philosophy Print design for JCSB is **editorial, not digital.** The goal is to look like a premium financial publication — think *Barron's* or a well-produced institutional white paper — not a website rendered on paper. **Core principles:** - **Ink has weight.** Use color as a structural device, not decoration. Large color fields establish zones; thin rules create hierarchy; white space carries as much meaning as content. - **Typography does the heavy lifting.** The contrast between Playfair Display (editorial authority) and DM Sans (clean utility) is the visual engine of the brand. Let font size, weight, and spacing replace the border-radius, shadow, and glow effects that belong in the web world. - **Restraint is sophistication.** Every element must justify its presence. No gradient meshes for visual interest alone. No pill badges. No shadow boxes or hover-state logic translated to static. - **Dark panels are used structurally, not decoratively.** A dark (`--blue-dark`) panel earns its place when it creates a distinct content zone — the opening problem statement, the closing CTA, a sidebar that contrasts a white body panel — not as ambient mood-setting. - **These documents accompany introductions, they don't replace them.** Print pieces are used alongside Brent's direct pitch — providing legitimacy and detail to a conversation already in progress. They build authority, not urgency. The design reflects this: measured, evidence-forward, not sales-forward. --- ## 2. Color Roles in Print All color tokens (hex values, names, rationale) are defined in the **Brand Foundation, Part 5**. This section defines how those tokens are used specifically in print. | Token | Print Role | |---|---| | `--green` | Primary text accent, section headers, ruled accents, CTA block background, checkmarks, bullet dots, eyebrow labels on light panels | | `--green-light` | Accent text and italic headline accents on **dark panels only** | | `--green-subtle` | Table row dividers, subtle field backgrounds | | `--blue-dark` | Dark panel backgrounds, primary body text, footer bars | | `--blue-mid` | Secondary headers, table column headers, eyebrow labels (secondary role on light panels) | | `--blue-body` | Body text on white panels, captions, descriptors | | `--blue-pale` | Light panel backgrounds, table alternate rows | | `--orange` | **Structural and urgency elements only** — see Orange Usage Policy below | | `--white` | Primary page/panel background | **Print color usage ratios:** - White/neutral: 60–70% of page area - Blue-dark panels: 20–25% (structural, zone-defining) - Green accents: 10–15% (headers, rules, CTA, callout blocks) - Orange: under 5% — structural and urgency language only --- ## 3. Orange Usage Policy (Print) The master orange policy is in the **Brand Foundation, Part 6.** This section defines the print-specific rules. Orange is a powerful accent because it is rare. In print, its role is **structural and urgency-specific only** — never decorative or general emphasis. ### Orange IS used for: - **Left-edge rule on dark panels** — the #1 structural signature of JCSB print materials - **Tension block border** (`border-left: 4px solid --orange`) when content specifically involves risk, legal exposure, compliance deadlines, or financial penalty language - **Urgency eyebrow labels on dark panels** when content is explicitly time-sensitive or risk-oriented (e.g., "Effective 2026 · Compliance Deadline") - **One structural hit per dark panel maximum** — never two orange elements in the same zone ### Orange is NOT used for: - General eyebrow labels on light/white panels (use `--green` or `--blue-mid`) - Signal dots (use `--green` on both dark and light panels) - Callout block borders carrying positive or value-forward content (use `--green`) - Any body text, descriptors, or captions - Decorative elements without a content rationale ### Campaign Distinction Orange text accents appear more frequently in **Takeover campaign** materials (existing plan holders) where risk agitation is the primary message. For **New Plan / Startup** materials, green carries all text accenting and orange remains structural-only. This is a meaningful brand signal between the two campaigns — preserve it. See Campaign Playbook for details. ### The underlying logic: Green is the brand's voice — it speaks for value, growth, and fiduciary partnership. Orange speaks for urgency and risk. When a reader sees orange, they should instinctively understand: *this is something to pay attention to.* That signal only works if orange is rare. --- ## 4. Typography in Print Typeface definitions and the full role hierarchy are in the **Brand Foundation, Parts 7 and 8.** This section defines print-specific sizes and weights. ### Print Type Scale *(Landscape letter: 1056×816px @ 96dpi)* | Role | Family | Size | Weight | Color | |---|---|---|---|---| | Panel headline (large) | Playfair Display | 32–42px | 700 | White or `--blue-dark` | | Section headline | Playfair Display | 22–28px | 700 | `--blue-dark` | | Italic subhead / accent | Playfair Display | 16–20px | 600 Italic | `--green` on light · `--green-light` on dark | | Eyebrow label | DM Sans | 10–12px | 600 | `--green` or `--blue-mid` on light · `--green-light` or `--orange` (urgency only) on dark | | Body copy | DM Sans | 12–14px | 300 | `--blue-dark` on light · `rgba(255,255,255,0.55)` on dark | | Caption / descriptor | DM Sans | 10–11px | 300–400 | `--blue-body` | | Disclosure | DM Sans | 8–9px | 300–400 | `rgba(255,255,255,0.28)` on dark · `--blue-body` on light | | Table header | DM Sans | 10px | 600, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.10em | White (on colored header) | | Table body | DM Sans | 11–12px | 300–400 | `--blue-dark` | ### Print Typography Rules - **Eyebrows are always all-caps DM Sans** with letter-spacing 0.16–0.18em - **Body weight is 300 (light), not 400** — reads as premium at print size - **Line height for body copy:** 1.60–1.65. For headlines: 1.15–1.25 - **Never use bold DM Sans for paragraph text** — bold DM Sans is reserved for item titles within lists or tables --- ## 5. Layout Architecture ### The Core Panel System Print documents use a **panel-based column layout**, not a card grid. Panels are structural — full-height zones that bleed to the page edge. **Standard panel types:** 1. **Dark Panel** (`--blue-dark` bg) — Problem/agitation, opening narrative, closing CTA. Orange left-edge rule. Always on the left or as a full-width band. 2. **White Panel** — Primary content zone. Clean white, check-items, signal rows, callout blocks. 3. **Light-fill Panel** (`--blue-pale` bg) — Sidebar role. Comparison tables, reference material, credential blocks. 4. **Full Dark Field + Inset White Document** — Entire page is dark; white content panel floats on the right two-thirds with a left drop-shadow. Left third is pure typographic composition — type against the dark field. Use for urgency-forward single-page pieces. ### Page-Level Layout Options | Layout | Use Case | |---|---| | Full-bleed dark / white split (60/40 or 50/50) | Cover pages, section openers | | Full-bleed dark / full white right (no dark footer) | Clean two-tone; contact/CTA in white panel | | White body left + dark sidebar right (65/35) | Detail pages with callout or stat box | | White body + pale-fill right panel (60/40) | Informational pages, comparison tables | | Three equal columns (33/33/33) | Feature sets, red-flag lists, model overview cards | | Full white with top colored band | Table pages, checklist pages | | Full dark field + inset white document (37/63) | Urgency-forward single-page pieces | **Do not use:** asymmetric card grids, floating cards with shadows, multi-colored background patchworks, or more than two background colors per page. ### Margins and Padding - Content inset: **36–44px** from page edges - Column gutter: **24–32px** - Internal panel padding: **32–44px** on dark panels, **28–36px** on white/pale panels - Eyebrow to headline gap: **10–14px** - Headline to body gap: **14–20px** - List item gap: **10–14px** --- ## 6. Structural Accent Elements ### Left-Edge Rule (Dark Panels) 4–5px vertical rule flush to the left edge of a dark panel or dark-field page, full height. **Always orange.** One per dark zone — never duplicated. ### Horizontal Rule / Divider `1px solid rgba(0,0,0,0.08)` on light · `rgba(255,255,255,0.07)` on dark. Used between check-item rows, table rows, section breaks, footer. **Never decorative.** ### Section Eyebrow Label `ALL CAPS · DM Sans 600 · 10–12px · letter-spacing 0.16em` - Color: `--green-light` on dark panels (`--orange` for urgency/deadline only) - Color: `--green` or `--blue-mid` on light panels ### Tension Block (Dark Panels — risk/urgency content only) - `border-left: 4px solid --orange` - Background: `rgba(243,141,12,0.06)` - Text: Playfair italic, 13–15px, `rgba(255,255,255,0.78)` - Use only for legal exposure, penalties, deadlines, or risk agitation content - One per dark panel maximum ### Callout Block (Light Panels — storytelling device, not CTA) A visual emphasis device that pulls a key fact, stat, or argument-defining statement out of the body. Content must be a **declarative statement** reinforcing the surrounding argument — never an imperative directed at the reader. **Test before using:** Does the copy contain an imperative verb directed at the reader? If yes → it is a CTA, belongs at the end only. If no → it is a callout, can appear in the body. **Light intensity (left-border only) — use in body:** - `border-left: 4px solid --green` - Background: `rgba(62,142,99,0.06)` - Text: Playfair italic, 13–15px, `--blue-dark` - Up to two per page, used sparingly **High intensity (filled block) — end-of-document CTA only:** - Background: `--green` - Text: DM Sans 600, white, 13–14px + DM Sans 300 subtitle `rgba(255,255,255,0.65)` 11px - `border-radius: 6–7px` - **One per document. Final page only.** ### Stat Panel A structural block presenting two or more key numbers. Effective as a full-width band between content sections or in a sidebar. - Background: `--blue-dark` - Numbers: Playfair Display 700, `--green-light`, 26–32px - Labels: DM Sans 300, `rgba(255,255,255,0.5)`, 10–11px - Internal columns separated by `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)` vertical rule ### Signal / Bullet Rows 3–5 narrative items. Dot: `7px circle, --green` on both dark and light panels. Text: DM Sans 300, 12–14px, bold key term at start of each item. `12–14px` gap between items, no visible border. ### Check-Item Row (Light Panels) `21–24px` green circle with white SVG checkmark. Title: DM Sans 600, 13px, `--blue-dark`. Descriptor: DM Sans 300, 11–12px, `--blue-body`. Divider: `1px solid #eef2f4`. SVG paths only — no emoji. ### Comparison Table Headers: DM Sans 600 uppercase 10px, `--blue-body` on `--green-subtle` border. JCSB column: `rgba(62,142,99,0.07)` bg. Check marks: `--green` 700 15px. Dashes: `#c0c8cc` 700 15px. Row dividers: `1px solid --green-subtle`. ### Footer Bar Background: `--blue-dark` · Height: 36–40px · Left: document title DM Sans 600 uppercase 9px letter-spacing 1.2px `rgba(255,255,255,0.65)` · Right: page number DM Sans 500 11px `rgba(255,255,255,0.4)` · Top border: `1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.07)`. ### Disclosure Strip `rgba(0,0,0,0.22–0.30)` on dark · ruled section at page bottom on light. DM Sans 300 8–9px. `rgba(255,255,255,0.28)` on dark · `--blue-body` on light. Line height: 1.45. --- ## 7. CTA Policy — One Per Document Print pieces accompany Brent's direct introduction. They build authority and provide detail to a conversation already in progress. This context drives strict CTA discipline. ### The rule: one CTA per printed document **End-of-document CTA (the only direct ask):** - Final page or final contact section only - High-intensity filled callout block (green background, white text) - Logical next step, not a pitch: "Schedule a plan review" over "Don't wait, act now" - One block. No secondary CTAs anywhere else in the document. **Cover / opening positioning statement (not a CTA):** - Frames the value being delivered inside the document - Written as a thesis statement or key principle, never an imperative - Gives the reader a reason to continue; does not ask them to act - Example: *"You shouldn't have to choose between keeping control of your 401(k) and spending your weekends reading ERISA regulations."* **Body callout blocks (storytelling, never CTAs):** - Declarative statements only — facts, principles, argument pivots - ✓ Good: "A 1% fee difference costs the average employee $34,000 over 30 years." - ✓ Good: "Good intentions are not a paper trail. ERISA expects documented, prudent process." - ✗ Bad: "Request a Fiduciary Gap Analysis today." (imperative — end of document only) - ✗ Bad: "Schedule a consultation to see what your plan is missing." (imperative — end of document only) ### Website vs. print distinction The website uses more aggressive CTA placement — appropriate for cold or warm digital traffic. In print, that approach erodes credibility. The reader is already in relationship with Brent; the document deepens trust and provides substance. It does not convert. --- ## 8. Background Treatments ### On Dark Panels 1. **Subtle mesh gradient** (optional): Two radial gradients, `rgba(62,142,99,0.12)` and `rgba(243,141,12,0.07)`, opposite corners, z-index 0. Atmosphere only. 2. **Grid texture** (optional): Crosshatch at 28–32px intervals, `rgba(255,255,255,0.018)`. Depth without noise. 3. **Background glyph** (sparingly): Large Playfair character at 220–320px, `rgba(255,255,255,0.025)`, off-center or bleeding off an edge. Covers and section openers only — never every page. ### On White/Light Panels No texture. Clean white only. Structure from typography and ruled lines, not background effects. `--blue-pale` as full column fills, never individual card backgrounds. --- ## 9.
