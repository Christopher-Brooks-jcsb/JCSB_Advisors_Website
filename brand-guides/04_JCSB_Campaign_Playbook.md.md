If your advisor is quiet, they aren't just "staying out of the way" — they are leaving you to bear fiduciary exposure without documentation, benchmarking, or guidance. Quiet is not the same as "handled." **The Solution:** A 3(21) co-fiduciary partner who provides a documented paper trail — IPS reviews, fee benchmarking, participant disclosures — to prove the business owner is acting in participants' best interests. **Tone:** Agitation to resolution. Name the risk clearly, then position JCSB as the documented process that removes it. Orange is permitted for urgency accents in this campaign. See Print Design Guide, Section 11. **Key proof points to reference:** - ERISA expects documented, prudent process — good intentions are not a paper trail - A 1% fee difference costs the average employee $34,000 over 30 years - If you can't show a documented decision-making process, you are personally exposed --- ### Campaign B — New Plan Setup (Startups & Growth Firms) **Target:** Business owners without a 401(k) plan considering starting one. **Primary Value:** Competitive talent retention and significant tax incentives, with a custom-architected plan rather than a bundled off-the-shelf product. **The "Why Us":** JCSB doesn't just sell a product — we architect a plan by selecting the right TPA and Recordkeeper for the client's specific needs and size. **Legislative Hook — SECURE Act 2.0:** - Up to **$5,000/year in startup tax credits** for the first three years of a new plan - Additional **$500/year credit** for plans with automatic enrollment - This means the federal government subsidizes a meaningful portion of first-year plan costs **Tone:** Aspiration to partnership. Lead with the opportunity (tax credits, talent edge), not the risk. Green carries all text accenting in this campaign — orange remains structural only. See Print Design Guide, Section 11. **Key proof points to reference:** - Top talent doesn't just look at salary — they look at stability and benefits - A custom-architected retirement plan signals that the company is invested in its people's future - SECURE Act 2.0 credits lower the barrier to entry significantly for small businesses --- ## Part 2: The 3(21) Fiduciary Framework This is the conceptual foundation for all business owner campaign materials. Use it to build explanations, website copy, comparison tables, and leave-behind content. ### The Framing: Collaborative Defense As a 3(21) advisor, JCSB serves as a **co-fiduciary.** The business owner remains the final decision-maker (retaining control) while JCSB provides the subject matter expertise and documented process required to satisfy ERISA standards. **The practical reality:** In practice, JCSB handles the research, benchmarking, and coordination. The business owner's role is to review and authorize. This is the **"Easy Button" for compliance** without losing **ownership of the plan.** ### Why 3(21) Is Better Than 3(38) (Discretionary) | Dimension | 3(21) — JCSB's Approach | 3(38) — Discretionary | |---|---|---| | Client control | Retained — owner reviews and approves all changes | Surrendered — advisor has full discretion | | Fiduciary status | Shared — both parties are co-fiduciaries | Transferred — but original liability never fully disappears | | Governance | Owner is informed and protected by expert partnership | Owner may be less informed about plan decisions | | Customization | Owner can request specific changes; JCSB implements | Changes go through advisor discretion | **Key framing:** Unlike a 3(38) advisor who takes full discretion (limiting the owner's input), the 3(21) approach allows the owner to retain full policy control. JCSB ensures the owner is never in the dark — providing the **"Informed Review"** that makes them legally protected because they have an expert partner helping them fulfill their duties. ### Vocabulary for the 3(21) / Fiduciary Context | Use This | Avoid This | Reasoning | |---|---|---| | Shared Fiduciary Burden | We take your liability | Accurate — implies partnership, not transfer | | Retained Policy Control | Full Discretion | Highlights that the client keeps authority | | Subject Matter Expertise | Investment Picks | Positions JCSB as high-level consultant | | Custom-Architected Plan | Turnkey / Bundled Solution | Distinguishes from generic providers | | Informed Review | Sign-off / Approval | Positions review as expertise-backed, not rubber stamp | | Co-Fiduciary | Fiduciary Manager | Accurate role description | | Documented Process / Paper Trail | Compliance | Concrete; ERISA-specific | | Fee Benchmarking | Cost Review | Technical specificity builds credibility | | Proactive Silence | Passive Management | Frames behind-the-scenes work as a benefit | | Safe of Mind | Peace of Mind | JCSB-specific phrase — use it intentionally | --- ## Part 3: Personal Wealth Advisory Campaign **Target:** Individuals and families (not business owners) considering a comprehensive financial planning relationship. **Primary Pain Point:** Fragmented finances — multiple accounts, advisors, and products that don't connect into a coherent plan. Often paired with the concern that their current "advisor" is really just a product salesman. **The Value Proposition:** A single CFP®-credentialed advisor who looks at the entire financial picture — not just investments — and builds a coordinated strategy across retirement income, insurance, taxes, and estate planning. ### Key Differentiators to Emphasize - **The CFP® Advantage:** Anyone can call themselves a financial advisor. Brent Turner's CFP® designation requires extensive education, examination, and ongoing ethics standards. This is the credential that separates advisors from representatives. - **IRMAA Analysis:** Including Medicare surcharge modeling in Roth conversion analysis is a meaningful technical differentiator — often overlooked by generalist advisors, but materially impactful for pre-retirees. - **Cash Flow Modeling:** Real-world income and spending alignment — not a Monte Carlo simulation handed over as a printout, but an ongoing planning conversation. - **Reporting and Visualization:** Professional software with real reports — not a brokerage statement. - **Local and named:** Clients have a specific person. Not a call center, not a rotating associate. ### Personal Wealth Campaign Tone Warmer and more editorial than the business owner campaign. Lead with the client's life and goals — not with risk, liability, or compliance. The urgency in this campaign comes from opportunity cost (years of uncoordinated planning) not from regulatory exposure. ### Vocabulary for the Personal Wealth Context | Use This | Avoid This | Reasoning | |---|---|---| | Holistic Financial Plan | Complete Package | Signals integration | | Retirement Income Strategy | Retirement Plan | Emphasizes distribution, not just accumulation | | Custom-Architected Portfolio | Managed Account | Signals intentional design | | Primarily Fee-Based | Fee-Only | Legally accurate | | Private Investments | Alternatives | More accessible; avoids jargon | | Portfolio Diversification | Higher Returns | Avoids promises | | Role Disclosure | Conflict of Interest | Neutral, professional tone | | Safe of Mind | Low Cost / Cheap | Focuses on value, not price | --- ## Part 4: Website Copy Pillars These are the approved draft copy blocks for key website sections.
