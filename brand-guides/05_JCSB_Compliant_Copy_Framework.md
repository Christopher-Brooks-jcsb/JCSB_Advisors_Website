\# JCSB Wealth Management — Compliant Copy Framework ### Addendum to Brand Foundation · Campaign Playbook *Last updated: May 2026 — Internal Use Only* --- ## How This Document Works This guide is an **addendum** to the Brand Foundation (`01_JCSB_Brand_Foundation`) and Campaign Playbook (`04_JCSB_Campaign_Playbook`). It does not redefine brand voice or campaign messaging — it provides a practical framework for writing copy that creates genuine engagement while meeting FINRA, SEC, and general securities marketing compliance standards. **Load this document alongside the Campaign Playbook** for any copy task: website sections, email campaigns, leave-behinds, social content, or any output where a reader needs to feel recognized before they'll take action. **The central problem this document solves:** Marketing frameworks like Hook/Story/Offer create real engagement — but many of their tactics rely on unsubstantiated claims, leading questions, or implied accusations that cannot survive compliance review in a registered investment advisory context. This framework translates the psychological mechanics of those approaches into forms that are both effective and defensible. --- ## Part 1: The Core Principle ### Recognition Over Accusation The most common compliance failure in financial services marketing is the **implied accusation**: copy that tells the reader something is wrong with their current situation before establishing any factual basis for that claim. Examples of implied accusation (non-compliant): - *"Most financial plans are missing three critical elements."* - *"Your advisor may not be telling you about IRMAA."* - *"Is your 401(k) costing you more than you think?"* - *"Find out what your current plan is missing."* Each of these asserts — or strongly implies — a factual claim about the reader's situation that we cannot substantiate. They also rely on fear or suspicion of an existing advisor relationship, which regulators view as manipulative. **Recognition-based copy does the same psychological work without the compliance exposure.** Instead of asserting that something is wrong, we describe a real planning concept with technical specificity. The reader recognizes whether it applies to them. We never tell them — they conclude it themselves. > **The governing rule:** We describe what exists in financial planning. The reader does the concluding. --- ## Part 2: The Three Copy Mechanics These are the compliant translations of the Hook / Story / Offer framework, adapted for a trust-based, multi-month sales cycle. --- ### Mechanic 1: The Recognition Hook **What it replaces:** The direct-response "hook" — a claim or question designed to create immediate tension. **The compliance problem with direct hooks:** They typically make unsubstantiated market claims ("most people..."), ask leading questions that imply a problem, or use fear-of-loss framing tied to the reader's current advisor or plan. **The compliant version:** Name a specific, real planning concept with enough technical precision that the right reader recognizes it as relevant to their situation — and the wrong reader self-excludes. The tension is created by recognition, not by assertion. **The test:** After reading the hook, could a reader who already has excellent planning say "yes, my plan addresses that" and move on satisfied? If yes, the hook is compliant. If the hook is designed to make everyone feel they have a problem, it isn't. #### Recognition Hook Formula ``` [Technical concept name] — because [one sentence explaining  why it matters for a specific life stage or situation]. ``` **Examples:** | Non-Compliant | Compliant Recognition Hook | |---|---| | *"Most retirees don't know about IRMAA."* | *"IRMAA-adjusted Roth conversion strategy — because Medicare surcharges can add thousands per year when RMD income isn't planned around them."* | | *"Is your 401(k) plan costing you more than you think?"* | *"Fee benchmarking under ERISA — because the standard of prudent process requires documented comparison, not just a belief that fees are reasonable."* | | *"Find out what your financial plan is missing."* | *"Business and personal finances coordinated together — because the decisions inside your company directly affect your personal tax position."* | | *"Your retirement plan might not be enough."* | *"Cash flow modeling through age 90 — because a retirement income strategy built around a projected account balance is a different document than one built around your actual spending."* | #### What Makes It Work The recognition hook works for the same psychological reason as a direct hook — it creates a "does this apply to me?" reaction — but it does so by **naming a real thing**, not by asserting a problem. The reader who doesn't know what IRMAA is learns why it matters. The reader who does know immediately asks whether their plan accounts for it. Neither conclusion was stated by us. --- ### Mechanic 2: The Illustrative Situation **What it replaces:** The "Customer Win" and "Relatable Struggle" story templates from direct-response frameworks. **The compliance problem with story templates:** First-person client quotes (even illustrative ones) require testimonial disclaimers and raise suitability concerns. Implied quotes ("I wish I'd done this two years ago") suggest specific outcomes. "Before/after" story structures imply that the previous situation was deficient. **The compliant version:** Describe the **planning concept** involved in a situation, then explain what the planning work entails and what it produces. The vignette is about the work, not about a client's transformation. #### Illustrative Situation Structure **Four required components — in this order:** 1. **The Audience Label** — who this situation is relevant to, described neutrally by life stage or circumstance (not by a problem they have) 2. **The Planning Concept** — a factual description of the financial planning concept at stake, written as information, not as a warning 3. **The Planning Work** — what we specifically do in this type of situation, in concrete terms 4. **The Output** — what the planning work produces: a document, a model, a framework — not an outcome or result **Required disclaimer on every illustrative situation:** > *Illustrative example only. Individual situations and outcomes will vary. This is not [tax / legal / investment] advice.* **Structure template:** ``` AUDIENCE LABEL [Life stage or situation descriptor — neutral, not problem-framing] THE PLANNING CONCEPT [Technical explanation of the financial planning concept involved.  Written as information. No claim that the reader's situation lacks this.] THE PLANNING WORK [What we specifically do in this type of engagement, in concrete terms. Use "in this type of situation, we..." not "we found that you were missing..."] THE OUTPUT [What the planning work produces — a document, model, framework, or plan. Not a result, not a promised outcome.] DISCLAIMER Illustrative example only. Individual situations and outcomes will vary. This is not [tax / legal / investment] advice. ``` #### Compliant vs. Non-Compliant Story Examples **Non-compliant (implied accusation, outcome promise):** > *"Sarah had been with her advisor for years, but nobody had ever mentioned IRMAA. When we ran the numbers, she was on track to pay $5,800 more per year in Medicare premiums than she needed to. We fixed that."* Problems: names a specific outcome, implies the previous advisor failed, uses "fix" (implies correction of a deficiency). **Compliant (planning concept + work + output):** > *"In pre-retirement planning for clients in their late 50s and early 60s, IRMAA thresholds are a meaningful consideration in Roth conversion modeling. The IRMAA surcharge is triggered by income — including RMD income — that exceeds certain Medicare brackets. In this type of situation, we build a multi-year Roth conversion model that layers in projected RMD trajectories alongside IRMAA bracket thresholds, identifying the conversion window where incremental conversions may reduce the lifetime tax exposure of the portfolio. This produces a documented, year-by-year conversion plan that the client can review, adjust, and track over time.* > > *Illustrative example only. Individual situations will vary. This is not tax advice.*" --- ### Mechanic 3: The Friction-Removing Offer **What it replaces:** The "Offer" element of HSO — the mechanism that converts attention and trust into action. **The compliance problem with direct-response offers:** Scarcity ("limited spots"), urgency ("this week only"), and loss-aversion framing ("don't miss this") are either unsubstantiated or manipulative in a regulated context. They also undermine the trust-based positioning JCSB has built through the rest of the page. **The compliant version:** Name and remove the actual friction points that prevent a warm prospect from taking the first step. In a trust-based, multi-month sales cycle, the barrier to the first meeting is **perceived risk of commitment**, not price or scarcity. Address that directly. **The legitimate concept to deploy:** Opportunity cost. This is a real, named financial planning concept — the value of an option not taken. It can be referenced honestly to create appropriate urgency without being manipulative, because it describes something real (planning decisions have time-sensitive windows) without asserting anything about the reader's specific situation. #### Friction-Removing Offer Structure State exactly what the first step is. Then explicitly state what it is not. Then name and remove each barrier. **Compliant urgency framing (uses opportunity cost correctly):** > *"Planning decisions have a concept called opportunity cost — the value of the option not taken. In financial planning, that often means the value of a decision that wasn't available in a window that has since closed. The complimentary review is designed to give you useful information about your situation — regardless of what you decide to do next."* **Non-compliant urgency framing:** > *"Don't wait — Roth conversion windows don't last forever."* > *"Every year you delay costs you money."* > *"Most people wish they'd called sooner."* The first version names a real concept and invites reflection. The second group asserts a problem and implies the reader is already losing — unsubstantiated and manipulative. #### The "What This Is Not" Device One of the most effective compliance-friendly conversion tools is explicitly stating what the first step is not. This removes perceived risk without making any claim about outcomes or urgency. **Template:** ``` What [the first step] is not: — A product presentation or pitch for a specific investment or insurance product — A requirement to bring any documents or prepare anything in advance — A commitment to move forward or engage our services — [Any other specific fear the target audience might have] [The first step] is designed to give you useful, honest information  about your situation — full stop. ``` This structure works because it directly addresses the unstated objections that prevent warm leads from booking. It is entirely factual, makes no claims about outcomes, and the contrast between "what it is" and "what it is not" creates clarity that sophisticated audiences (HNW, business owners, pre-retirees) actively appreciate. --- ## Part 3: Self-Identification Copy Self-identification copy is a specific technique for the hero or opening section of any page or document. Instead of asserting that the reader has a problem, it asks the reader to identify which stage or situation applies to them — producing the same "this is about me" recognition effect without any claim. ### Rules for Self-Identification Copy 1. **Frame by life stage or circumstance, not by problem.** "Approaching retirement (ages 50–65)" not "not sure your savings will last." 2. **Use neutral, descriptive language.** The reader is in a situation, not in trouble. 3. **Each option should stand on its own.** A reader in none of the situations should feel no pressure to select one anyway. 4. **Never include a "none of these" option that implies the reader is missing out.** The self-identification is a routing tool, not a sales funnel. ### Self-Identification Option Templates ``` Option format: [Neutral life stage / situation label] [One sentence describing what they're thinking about —  not what they're doing wrong] ``` **Examples:** | Non-Compliant | Compliant Self-Identification | |---|---| | *"I have accounts but no plan"* | *"Building personal wealth alongside a career or business"* | | *"My advisor isn't proactive"* | *"Looking for a second opinion or an independent review"* | | *"I don't know if I can retire"* | *"Approaching retirement and thinking through the income side"* | | *"My company plan has no one watching it"* | *"A business owner with a retirement plan to maintain and employees to serve"* | --- ## Part 4: The Second Opinion Frame The "second opinion" is one of the highest-value concepts in JCSB's marketing — it addresses existing advisor relationships without attacking them and provides a clear, low-friction entry point. But it requires careful framing. ### Compliant Second Opinion Language **The medical analogy is compliant and effective:** > *"A second opinion in financial planning is similar to a second opinion in medicine — it's not a statement about your current provider. It's a way to verify that your plan is accounting for the dimensions most relevant to your situation. The complimentary review may confirm that what you have is well-suited to where you are. That's a worthwhile conversation on its own."* This framing works because: - It explicitly does not criticize the existing advisor - It acknowledges the possibility that the existing plan is adequate - It positions the review as information-gathering, not competitive displacement - It reduces the perceived betrayal of seeking a second opinion **Non-compliant second opinion language:** | Avoid | Why | |---|---| | *"Find out what your current advisor isn't telling you."* | Implies the existing advisor is withholding information — unsubstantiated accusation | | *"See how your plan stacks up."* | Implies a comparison that may be unfavorable — unsubstantiated | | *"Get a real second opinion from a CFP®."* | "Real" implies the existing advisor is not genuine — disparagement | | *"What questions should you be asking your advisor?"* | Designed to create suspicion — implied accusation framing | ### The Question to Ask Yourself (Compliant Version) One effective and compliant device is presenting questions the reader can ask *themselves* — not questions to use against their advisor. The distinction matters to compliance. **Compliant:** > *"A few questions worth reflecting on: When did you last review your retirement income sequencing? Has your IRMAA exposure been modeled alongside your Roth conversion strategy? Is your estate plan current relative to your assets and beneficiary designations? These are the kinds of planning dimensions we address in a complimentary review."* **Non-compliant:** > *"Ask your advisor: Has my IRMAA been modeled? Can you show me the fee benchmarking on my 401(k)? When did we last update my IPS?"* The first presents planning concepts for the reader's self-reflection. The second is designed to create an adversarial dynamic with the existing advisor. --- ## Part 5: Vocabulary — Compliant Substitutions This table supplements the master vocabulary table in the Brand Foundation (Part 9). If any answer is "yes," revise before submitting. **1. Does this make a factual claim about the reader's situation without a factual basis?** > If the copy asserts something about the reader's current plan, advisor, or financial position without the reader having told us — it's an unsubstantiated claim. The reader must be doing the concluding, not us. **2. Is there an implied accusation against an existing advisor, firm, or product?** > If the copy is designed to create suspicion, distrust, or dissatisfaction with an existing relationship — even indirectly — it is disparagement. The second opinion frame must explicitly not be an attack. **3. Does this promise or imply a specific outcome, return, or result?** > Any language that implies specific financial results — including comparative language like "better returns" or "lower fees" — requires substantiation. If we don't have the data to back it up, don't say it. **4. Would a reader with a well-functioning existing plan feel manipulated after reading this?** > The recognition hook is designed to help the right reader self-identify. If it's designed to make everyone feel they have a problem — including people who don't — it's manipulative, not recognitive. --- ## Part 7: Quick Reference — The Compliant HSO Pattern This is the one-page summary of how Hook / Story / Offer translates to the JCSB context. Post this at the top of any copy brief. ### Hook → Recognition **Goal:** Create "does this apply to me?" without asserting that it does. **Method:** Name a specific, real planning concept with technical precision. Let the reader self-identify. They do the concluding. **Test:** Can a reader with a well-functioning plan read this and feel appropriately excluded? If yes, it's recognition. If everyone should feel implicated, it's accusation. --- ### Story → Illustrative Situation **Goal:** Build credibility and demonstrate planning depth through concrete examples. **Method:** Describe the planning concept involved, the work we do, and the output it produces. Never a client transformation. Always an illustrative disclaimer. **Test:** Does the vignette describe what we do, or does it describe what was wrong before we arrived? If the latter, revise. --- ### Offer → Friction Removal **Goal:** Make the first step feel small, safe, and clearly worth taking. **Method:** State exactly what the first step is and is not. Name and remove the specific barriers (commitment, prep, pressure). Use opportunity cost — a real financial concept — as the only urgency mechanism. **Test:** Does the urgency have a named, factual basis? Or is it manufactured scarcity/fear? If the latter, remove it. --- *JCSB Wealth Management · Brent Turner, CFP® · Internal Use Only* *This document extends 01_JCSB_Brand_Foundation and 04_JCSB_Campaign_Playbook.* *Load Brand Foundation first, then the relevant medium or campaign guide, then this document for any copy task.*
